# EXP-017 Audited Generation-Time Behavioral Pilot

## Motivation

EXP-018 provided independent representation-level evidence that a task-derived
direction can move held-out Qwen hidden states in a target-directed way. Whether
that movement produces task-specific generation behavior was still untested.
EXP-017 was a frozen, controlled test of that remaining question; it was not a
test of reasoning improvement or target-task conversion.

## Audit History

The original EXP-017 preregistration preceded Research Audit v1. The audit
identified construction coupling in historical centroid/nearest-centroid
results and a common-translation specificity problem for RSM/IVS. EXP-018 then
passed independent transition validation but failed relational validation. A
post-audit amendment retained only Qwen L16 at beta 0.75, a matched-norm random
control, and an opposite control. Task 042 independently verified the exact
KV-cache hook semantics before the official behavioral pilot. This sequence is
an evidentiary correction, not a success narrative.

## Frozen Design

The model was `Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B`. The four conditions were
`NO_INTERVENTION`, `TASK_REAL`, `MATCHED_RANDOM`, and `OPPOSITE`. Every
intervention used layer 16 and beta 0.75. The frozen source-to-target mappings
were logic to causality, causality to logic, analogy to definition, and
definition to analogy. The behavioral evaluation used all 80 frozen EXP-011D
items with deterministic short-answer generation (`do_sample=False`,
`max_new_tokens=32`) and boundary-aware scoring.

## Steering Direction Construction

For each group, a centroid was fit only from its six controlled EXP-003 prompts
at Qwen L16. The task vector was `centroid_target - centroid_source`. No
EXP-011D prompt, answer, output, score, or label contributed to centroid
fitting. One deterministic matched-norm random direction per frozen transition
was reused across matching items and decode steps; the opposite vector was the
exact negative task vector. No vectors or hidden states were persisted.

## Generation Hook

The hook targeted `model.model.layers[16]` (`Qwen3DecoderLayer`) after its
block output and before the next block. It modified only
`hidden_states[:, -1, :]`: once for the final prompt token at prefill and once
for the current token at every cached decode step. The zero-vector diagnostic
was exactly behavior-neutral; removing the hook restored baseline output; no
previous KV-cache entries were mutated.

## Behavioral Dataset and Scoring

The EXP-011D baseline and all answer vocabulary were held fixed. The primary
outcome was source-task accuracy; it is not a target-sensitive measure. Frozen
collateral diagnostics were output-token count, empty-answer rate, adjacent
exact repetition, and malformed/non-short-answer rate. No LLM judge or post
hoc rescore was used.

## Baseline Replication

`NO_INTERVENTION` exactly reproduced EXP-011D: overall accuracy was 0.750;
logic 0.750; causality 0.950; analogy 0.450; and definition 0.850. Thus the
baseline replication classification is `EXACT`.

## Primary TASK vs RANDOM Result

TASK and matched random had identical overall accuracy (0.6375; difference
0.0000). Group differences (TASK minus RANDOM) were -0.15 for logic, -0.05
for causality, -0.20 for analogy, and +0.40 for definition. At the item level,
TASK was correct while RANDOM was incorrect on 11 items, and the reverse held
on 11 items; 40 were both correct and 18 both incorrect. Correctness differed
on 22/80 items (27.5%). The group pattern is mixed rather than a stable
task-directed advantage.

## TASK vs Baseline and RANDOM vs Baseline

TASK was 0.1125 below baseline: 13 baseline-correct items became TASK-incorrect
and 4 baseline-incorrect items became TASK-correct; 47 were both correct and
16 both incorrect. All four source groups decreased. This loss is not target
conversion.

RANDOM was also 0.1125 below baseline: 11 baseline-correct items became
RANDOM-incorrect and 2 baseline-incorrect items became RANDOM-correct; 49 were
both correct and 18 both incorrect. Definition was especially degraded under
RANDOM, which is important for interpretation of the primary comparison.

## Opposite-Direction Result

TASK was 0.025 below OPPOSITE overall. TASK was correct while OPPOSITE was
incorrect on 12 items, whereas OPPOSITE was correct while TASK was incorrect
on 14; 39 were both correct and 15 both incorrect. Group directions were mixed.
The opposite condition therefore does not establish directional behavioral
specificity.

## Collateral Diagnostics

The collateral classification is `MIXED_WITH_ARTIFACT`. In particular,
MATCHED_RANDOM had overall malformed rate 0.20 and definition malformed rate
0.55, versus 0.0625 and 0.20 for TASK. Thus the Definition advantage of TASK
over RANDOM is partly contaminated by condition-specific generic output
disruption. TASK's deficits relative to RANDOM in the other three groups show
that the total record is not reducible to one simple corruption pattern, but it
does not yield clean task-specific evidence.

## Behavioral Effect Judgment

`behavioral_effect = FAILED`; `representation_behavior_link = NOT_SUPPORTED`;
and group consistency is `MIXED`. The frozen stop rule yields
`STOP_AFTER_NEGATIVE`.

## Interpretation and Claim Boundaries

The maximum defensible conclusion is: “Although the task-derived direction
strongly altered held-out hidden-state representations in EXP-018, the same
direction did not produce a stable task-specific behavioral advantage over a
matched-norm random perturbation in the frozen generation-time pilot.”

Different activation perturbations changed item-level outcomes, but that fact
alone does not establish task-direction-specific behavioral control. EXP-017
does not support claims that steering improves reasoning, converts the source
task to the target task, guarantees behavioral transition from representation
transition, makes opposite steering produce opposite target behavior, validates
IVS as behavioral safety, or validates EXP-016 safe-control roles.

## Limitations

- One behavioral model and an 80-item controlled benchmark.
- Source-task accuracy is not a target-sensitive behavior metric.
- One primary intervention layer and beta.
- Output effects can depend on intervention timing.
- No target-behavior classifier was defined.
- No layer or beta post-hoc search was performed after the negative result.

## Stop Rule Outcome

The task direction did not outperform the matched-norm random control on the
frozen overall outcome, and the group-level contrast was mixed with a material
random-condition artifact. The preregistered outcome is to record the negative
result and stop rather than tune layers or betas.
