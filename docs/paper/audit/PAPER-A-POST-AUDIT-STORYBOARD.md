# Paper-A Post-Audit Storyboard

Status: `PA_NOVELTY_002_POST_AUDIT_STORYBOARD`
Scope: EXP-021 through EXP-027 only. No manuscript prose is written here.

## Central Question

`Does shared depth-distance-associated fixed-readout compatibility imply shared source/target organization and simple recalibratability across models?`

## One-Sentence Answer

`In three prospectively registered 1B-class language models, all three show positive depth-distance-associated fixed-readout compatibility structure, but source/target organization and low-dimensional recalibratability combine differently, with Llama matching Qwen on target dominance and OLMo on supported LOW-D recovery.`

## Primary Contribution

`A preregistered three-model empirical dissociation of cross-depth fixed-readout compatibility dimensions: distance-associated structure is common, while source/target organization and simple recalibratability are not determined by that common structure.`

## Secondary Contributions

1. `Multidimensional operational characterization of cross-depth fixed-readout compatibility.`
2. `A registered three-model empirical profile comparison, including a prospective third-model test.`

## Methodological Contributions

- `Carrier comparability control across Qwen, OLMo, and Llama final-norm/hidden-state semantics.`
- `Frozen source-family/bootstrap/routing governance and prospective registration.`

## Established Prior-Art Background

- Cross-layer representational mismatch is established.
- Fixed-readout degradation across layers is established.
- Layer-distance-associated transfer degradation is established.
- Source x target layer matrices are established.
- Layer-specific measurement frames can differ.
- Probe failure does not prove information absence.

## Main Results Sequence

1. Begin with established mismatch and decodability/causal-role boundaries.
2. Introduce the unresolved question: is fixed-readout compatibility one scalar degradation axis or multiple separable dimensions?
3. Define three operational dimensions: distance-associated structure, source/target organization, simple recalibratability.
4. Report EXP-026: Qwen and OLMo both support distance structure, but Qwen is target-dominant with LOW-D not supported, while OLMo is source-dominant with LOW-D supported.
5. Report EXP-027: Llama is target-dominant and LOW-D supported, prospectively breaking the simple Qwen/OLMo organization-to-recalibratability mapping.
6. State the bounded conclusion: a scalar distance axis is insufficient; multiple compatibility dimensions can combine differently across models.

## Key Limitations

- Three controlled 1B-class models; no generalization claim.
- Controlled panel/template data; no natural-distribution claim.
- SDI/LOW-D are operational project-specific metrics.
- No causal architecture or training-history attribution.
- No practical deployment or industry impact demonstrated.
- SemRF is a strong measurement-frame prior-art collision.

## Why It Matters

Allowed methodological implications only:

- `DIRECTLY_SUPPORTED`: fixed readouts and layerwise probes are not automatically portable across depth.
- `METHODOLOGICAL_IMPLICATION`: cross-depth measurement-frame assumptions should be audited before interpreting apparent motion or decay.
- `METHODOLOGICAL_IMPLICATION`: representation-alignment diagnostics should not assume a single scalar compatibility axis.
- `FUTURE_APPLICATION`: profiles may inform probe/interface design, but this is not demonstrated.
- `SPECULATION`: no industry/behavioral/transport impact is claimed.
