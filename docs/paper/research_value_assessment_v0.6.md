# Research Value Assessment v0.6

## Undergraduate Research Value

The project has strong undergraduate-research value because it preserves a full
evidence trail: initial observations, replication, audit, independent controls,
and a recorded negative behavioral test. It demonstrates careful experimental
revision rather than result selection.

## Preprint or Workshop Readiness

The current record can support a cautious methods-and-evidence-audit preprint
or workshop-style submission if claims remain narrow and all negative results
are foregrounded. It is not positioned as a new state-of-the-art steering
method.

## Full Conference Limitations

Major limits are two small models, 24 controlled representation prompts, one
behavioral model, a finite-answer 80-item benchmark, no target-sensitive
behavioral outcome, and no evidence of task-specific generation control. The
negative result also limits claims about practical intervention value.

## Strongest Novelty Angle

The strongest angle is evidence auditing: separating construction-coupled
representation metrics from held-out validation, then separating
representational steerability from behavioral controllability. The project is
also strengthened by treating failed relational and behavioral validations as
central findings.

## Biggest Threats to Validity

The remaining threats are small hand-designed data, centroid-vector dependence
on a narrow prompt distribution, limited model scale, finite-answer scoring,
and the absence of a target-sensitive behavioral measure. Independent
representation validation does not eliminate these limits.

## Evidence Most Likely to Increase Scientific Value

The highest-value new evidence would be a predeclared, independently evaluated
target-sensitive behavioral outcome with appropriate generic-perturbation
controls. It should test whether the observed hidden-state movement has a
behavioral correlate without post-hoc layer or beta tuning.
